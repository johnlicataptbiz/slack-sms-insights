- "what works" highlights from real booked threads

Source basis:

## Pattern 1: Validate first, then tighten the business picture

Real thread sequence:

> "Love it!! Year 3 is usually where things start getting interesting haha. Are you guys fully cash based right now, or hybrid? And is EndGame more general ortho, or do you have a specific niche you focus on??"

Then:

> "That's a great setup. A lot of our training and programs are very specialized around performance based care, athletes, and active adults, so it sounds like there could be strong alignment there. Are you guys currently maxed on your own schedules, or starting to feel the need to bring another clinician in?"

What works:
- quick enthusiasm
- specific acknowledgment of the lead's situation
- immediate move into business-stage qualification
- no hard pitch yet

Use when:
- the lead gives real context
- the lead already sounds serious
- the agent needs to build momentum without rushing to the call

## Pattern 2: Diagnose the bottleneck in business language

Real thread sequence:

> "Trying to prepare for this year. Have a new clinician working part time for us trying to build them up with case load Close to max on our schedules trying to leverage a little more time to do back end and marketing to get more people into the office"

Jack replies:

> "It sounds like you are close to firing on all cylinders already Matt! The good news is, when clinics hit that \"almost maxed\" phase, it's usually not a demand issue, right? Thats harder to solve- haha. It's more tightening up systems so the new clinician fills up without you guys staying stuck in treatment."

What works:
- he reframes the situation into a recognizable growth phase
- he makes the problem more solvable
- he sounds like he has seen this before
- the humor is light and natural

Use when:
- the lead describes operational strain
- the lead is near capacity
- the agent wants to turn scattered details into a strategic picture

## Pattern 3: Pitch the strategy call as clarity, not pressure

Real thread sequence:

> "This is exactly what we help performance based cash clinics map out on a strategy call. We'd look at how to consistently fill that new clinician's caseload, how to free up your time without stalling growth, and what a clean path to scaling beyond owner capacity could look like for you guys."

Then:

> "It's not something we offer everyone since we stay pretty specialized, but you two seem like a strong fit. And even if nothing else, the clarity alone would likely make this year smoother."

What works:
- the call is framed as practical working time
- "specialized fit" increases perceived relevance
- "clarity alone" lowers pressure

Use when:
- the lead has already shown enough fit
- the agent wants to introduce the call without sounding salesy

## Pattern 4: Book collaboratively, not mechanically

Real thread sequence:

> "If you're open to it, what weekdays tend to work best? AM or PM better? Happy to get you guys connected."

Then later:

> "Yes, definitely most impactful to have you both on!! Great plan. Let's go ahead and lock in a Wednesday morning in early March so it's on the calendar and you two can confirm together. If anything needs to change, you can just use the reschedule link in the invite."

What works:
- he does not force a slot too early
- he makes the placeholder feel safe
- he keeps momentum while respecting the lead's decision process

Use when:
- multiple decision-makers are involved
- the lead is warm but needs coordination

## Pattern 5: Handle price questions without sounding evasive

Real thread from Kenny:

> "Great question Kenny! The strategy call itself is completely free. It's a deep dive into where you're at, what you're building, and what a realistic path to turning this into something substantial could look like. If it makes sense and we genuinely believe we can help you accelerate that transition, we'll walk you through what working together would look like and answer any questions around investment then."

What works:
- answers the literal question first
- explains the purpose of the call
- signals that pricing depends on fit/stage
- stays calm and non-defensive

Use when:
- the lead asks cost before booking
- the agent needs a non-pushy pricing answer

## Pattern 6: Stay useful after the lead is already booked

Real support snippets from the same booked thread:

> "Haha oops yes!! I am so sorry I totally meant to add that I will resend it right now!!"

> "Check your spam- I think the email might come from \"Kajabi\" - let me know if you still don't see it"

> "Amazing!! So sorry for all the trouble you had accessing it!"

What works:
- Jack stays human after the booking
- he solves the issue instead of switching to robotic admin mode
- the relationship continues past the calendar link

## What the agent should learn from these threads

- Jack earns the call by diagnosing the stage, not by blasting urgency.
- He mirrors the lead's business reality back to them in simpler language.
- He usually moves from:
  1. asset/context
  2. forced-choice qualification
  3. diagnosis/reframe
  4. strategy call as practical clarity
  5. easy scheduling

- The strongest Jack messages feel:
  - specific
  - lightly playful
  - strategically useful
  - not polished to death
